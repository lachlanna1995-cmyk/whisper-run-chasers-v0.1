# STAR BASE: DEAD ORBIT — Progression and Matchmaking Rules

These rules are mandatory for every future design, coding, balance, economy, live-ops, and monetization pass.

## Core progression
- Progression must be earned through normal play.
- Players advance by deploying, surviving, completing objectives, recovering resources, upgrading the base, and improving combat performance.
- Do not sell progression skips, upgrade materials, weapons, armor, stats, extra damage, extra health, faster research, matchmaking advantages, or premium currencies that convert into power.
- Do not stack multiple stores, currencies, passes, boosts, crates, bundles, subscriptions, or offer walls.
- Launch monetization is cosmetic/supporter-only.

## Time ceiling
- A normal, competent player should be able to reach the current seasonal progression ceiling in roughly 35 to 50 hours of active play.
- Progression pacing can be tuned using server configuration after real retention data exists.
- Do not use artificial multi-day timers to stretch the progression path.
- Do not increase required play time just to create monetization pressure.

## Matchmaking before the ceiling
- Match primarily by progression band, party size, region/latency, and recent combat performance.
- Protect new players from veteran accounts.
- Never include purchase history or money spent in matchmaking.
- New or returning players should receive a short calibration period before aggressive skill matching begins.

## Endgame / top-tier SBMM
- Once a player reaches the current top progression band, progression differences should stop deciding match quality.
- Top-tier players enter skill-based matchmaking.
- Initial skill seeding should consider multiple signals instead of one statistic:
  - objective contribution
  - exfil/survival rate
  - damage efficiency
  - accuracy
  - wins and losses
  - opponent strength
  - team contribution
- Do not optimize around kill/death ratio alone.
- Use broad hidden skill bands rather than forcing every match toward exact 50/50 outcomes.
- Allow the search range to widen gradually when queue times increase.
- Prioritize stable latency and reasonable queue time over microscopic skill precision.

## Anti-smurf / anti-abuse
- Rapidly move unusually strong new accounts out of beginner matchmaking.
- Use performance trends, not a single exceptional match.
- Do not punish genuinely new or weak players because they had one lucky round.
- Party matchmaking should consider the strongest players in the group enough to prevent deliberate low-skill queue abuse.

## Bots
- Bots are allowed for onboarding, training, empty-server backfill, and low-population protection.
- Bots must never masquerade as real humans in ranked results.
- Bot presence should be disclosed in match information where practical.
- At high-skill/top-tier matchmaking, prefer real players and use bots only when required to keep the mode playable.

## Monetization
- Launch with one straightforward supporter/cosmetic product if monetization is enabled.
- Cosmetic content may include skins, base appearance, banners, insignia, effects, and other visual-only items.
- Paid items cannot alter hitboxes, visibility, recoil, weapon behavior, movement speed, resource recovery, matchmaking, progression rate, or combat power.
- No loot boxes.
- No pay-to-win.
- No pay-to-catch-up.

## Swarm instruction
Any agent proposing a new economy, progression, matchmaking, monetization, or live-ops feature must explain how it follows these rules. If it conflicts, reject it instead of silently modifying these rules.
